# Solving linear inequalities

An inequality compares two expressions that are not equal. The symbols are:

- `<` means "is less than", for example 3 < 5.
- `>` means "is greater than", for example 7 > 2.
- `≤` means "is less than or equal to".
- `≥` means "is greater than or equal to".

A linear inequality in one variable has the variable to the first power, for
example x + 4 > 9 or 2x − 1 ≤ 7.

## Solving

We solve a linear inequality the same way as a linear equation, with one
important rule:

1. You may add or subtract the same number on both sides.
2. You may multiply or divide both sides by the same positive number.
3. If you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, you must
   reverse the inequality sign. For example, from −2x < 6 we get x > −3.

## Worked example

Solve 3x − 4 ≤ 11.

- Add 4 to both sides: 3x ≤ 15.
- Divide both sides by 3 (a positive number, so the sign stays): x ≤ 5.
- Answer: every number less than or equal to 5 is a solution, for example 5, 0 and −2.

## Showing the solution on a number line

For x ≤ 5 draw a filled dot at 5 (5 is included) and shade the line to the
left. For x > 5 draw an empty circle at 5 (5 is not included) and shade to
the right.
